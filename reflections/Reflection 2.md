I think the main idea to remember with this text is that the process of designing/creating is a much more back and forth conversation than people realize. This is because designing something, no matter the skill level, is unpredictable. The materials "talk back" with the designer, meaning that they often produce results that are unexpected or unwanted, which isn't always a bad thing. Some unexpected results can be an even better outcome than expected. These unexpected results force the designers to think of new ways to interact with the materials and reflect on their work. I noticed this is true through my prototyping of pong. I had originally tried to fix a simple problem so I could start thinking of ways to make the game more playful, which led me to experiment and somehow create a game way more fun and playful than my original idea. This isn't an isolated event and I have noticed it before through my limited experience playing around with making games. I think a big part of the reason my games almost never end up like my ideas is because of my lack of technical skill, but there is always a conversation with materials no matter the skill level.
My favourite idea from this reading is when the author talks about how the creator might not always understand what they have created. This part stuck with me because sometimes I feel fraudulent when I make something that I don't understand and it's good to know that it is just a part of the creative process.
