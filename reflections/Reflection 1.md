The Idea of rules is to follow them. Normally, breaking a rule means that you are doing something wrong. What I found interesting in this reading is how the rigidity of rules is challenged. Here rules are meant to be broken or altered, because it can completely alter a games experience, whether that be good or bad. Taking Pippin's class in my first year showed me this firsthand, when we had to come of with our own unique variation of a basic game he created. Breaking the rules led me to create one of my favourite projects i've done.
A part that resonated with me is when he speaks on the rules of video games specifically, calling them "literally encoded and largely unchangeable once the game is released"(25). Thinking about it, I can't remember a time where I've gone out of my way to break some of the fundamental rules of video games (outside of when I am assigned to do so). Sure I push the boundaries of games and look for ways to exploit and break them, but I've never really looked at the things that can not be changed through gameplay and asked myself what would happen if I changed those rules.
He also compares bending the rules to childlike whimsy, which I completely agree with. I find there is something interesting about approaching games with that same curiosity. On one hand, you can play a game and experience what the devellopper wants you to, learning whatever the mooral of that game is. on the other hand you can break the game and you are very likely to find and learn so much more.
